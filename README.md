# Speed vs Value vs Store
### What drives Gen Z's festive shopping channel choice in Kolkata

**MBA Marketing Project | Marketing & Business Analytics | IQ City United World School of Business | 2026**

> **Answer in one line:** 72% of Gen Z shoppers in Kolkata put **value or quality first**, against only **6.5% who put speed first**. Brands should lead festive campaigns with offers and quality, and use fast delivery as a last-minute add-on, not the main pitch.

## Business question

Quick commerce sells speed, yet recent industry surveys suggest festive buyers weigh value and quality more. Does this hold for Gen Z in Kolkata and nearby towns, and which channel wins their festive spend?

1. Which channel do Gen Z shoppers use most for festive purchases, and does it differ by area?
2. Does speed or value drive their channel choice?
3. Which customer segments exist, and how should a fashion brand target them?

## Method

| Step | What I did |
|---|---|
| Collect | Google Form survey of 200 Gen Z respondents (18–26) across Kolkata core, suburbs and other towns, Oct–Nov 2026 |
| Clean | Removed duplicates and impossible answers |
| Analyse | Excel (PivotTables, COUNTIFS, correlation), chi-square tests, basic Python |
| Segment | Rule-based customer segments |
| Recommend | Marketing actions linked to each segment, with retail KPIs |

## Key findings

1. **Value and quality beat speed.** Price/value (39.5%) and quality (32.5%) both outrank speed (6.5%). The order matches published industry benchmarks.
2. **Channel use splits three ways.** Brand Store 28.5%, Quick Commerce 28%, Online Marketplace 28%, Local Market 15.5%.
3. **Location matters.** Channel mix differs significantly by area (chi-square, p = 0.02). Quick commerce is 35.9% in the Kolkata core but 14.9% in other towns, while local markets reach 23.4% outside the core.
4. **Quick commerce owns the last-minute window, but few will pay for speed.** 57% of its users bought within 48 hours, yet only 18% would pay extra for 10-minute delivery. It also has the highest average spend (₹5,032) and satisfaction (4.0/5).
5. **Instagram Reels leads influence** (33%), ahead of friends (21%) and YouTube (20%).

## Customer segments

| Segment | Share | Avg. spend | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explorers | 51% | ₹4,747 | No single dominant pattern |
| Value Hunters | 32% | ₹4,664 | Price/value first, discount sensitivity 4–5 |
| Brand Loyalists | 15% | ₹3,945 | Brand loyalty 4–5, main channel is a brand store |
| Speed Seekers | 2% | ₹7,938 | Speed first, bought within 48 hours (only 4 respondents, a niche to test) |

## Recommendations

| Lever | Action | Target segment | KPIs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Offer & pricing | Festive bundle at a clear set price (e.g. 3 items for one saving) | Value Hunters | Conversion, ABV, UPT |
| Content & social | Reels with real customers and a 48-hour gift countdown | Explorers | Reel views, click to store, conversion |
| Channel | Pre-festive store visit pass with an early styling slot | Brand Loyalists | Footfall, conversion, ABV |

Position speed as a convenience for the 18% who will pay for it, not as the headline.

## Limitations

- Convenience sample of 200 respondents, not the whole market
- Spend and priorities are self-reported
- Covers one festive window (Durga Puja to Diwali)
- Small groups (other towns, non-binary respondents, Speed Seekers) are directional only
- Benchmark surveys allowed several priorities; mine asked for one top priority, so compare direction, not exact percentages

**Next steps:** repeat in the wedding season, add observed data (footfall, app orders), and pilot one recommendation.
